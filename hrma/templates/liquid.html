Solver Input Scope

Read this before you tune the form: these inputs define the engine configuration and are always read directly by the solver

  • Fuel type, Oxidizer type (panel 1)
  • Mixture ratio O/F (panel 2)
  • Target thrust, Chamber pressure (panel 3)
  • Injector type (panel 4)
  • Cooling type (panel 6)

Most other numeric fields now reach the solver as range-checked overrides. Fields the solver does not use are flagged next to the input after every run, with the reason: reference only, transient not modelled yet, or computed by the solver. A flagged field is recorded with the design and reaches the reports and exports, but it does not change the computed result. Values the solver sizes itself, such as the throat diameter and the injection velocities, are shown next to your entry so you can compare them.

Motor Cross-Section

What this shows: Axial cross-section of the engine, drawn to scale from the solver geometry (all dimensions in mm)

  • Chamber wall: Combustion chamber shell and head end; hover shows wall thickness and bore diameter
  • Injector plate: Face plate with type-specific internals (showerhead, impinging, coaxial, swirl or pintle)
  • Nozzle: True convergent-divergent contour (conical, Rao or bell) with wall offset
  • Dimension lines and throat station: Chamber length, chamber bore, throat and exit diameters
  • Centerline: Dash-dot axis of revolution; the section is mirrored about it
  • No fuel grain or liner: Both propellants are liquid, so the chamber is empty between the injector face and the throat

Performance Analysis Dashboard

What this shows: Fuel and oxidizer mass flow rates and the pressure distribution through the feed system, injector and chamber.

Design Exports

What you get: Manufacturing and simulation files generated from the current solver geometry

  • OpenRocket .eng: Constant-thrust motor file; burn time is taken from the "Maximum Burn Duration" input
  • Technical drawings PDF: Multi-page dimensioned drawing set with title block
  • DXF: 2D manufacturing profile (inner flow contour and chamber)
  • STEP: Parametric solids (chamber, nozzle contour, injector plate) plus assembly, zipped
  • Complete package: STL, DXF, drawings PDF, STEP, .eng and geometry JSON in one archive
  • Excel workbook: Performance, geometry, injector and feed-system sheets

Engine Subsystems

What this shows: Parametric analysis across different mixture ratios and chamber pressures

  • X-axis: Mixture Ratio (O/F) - oxidizer to fuel mass ratio
  • Y-axis: Performance metrics (Thrust, Isp, chamber pressure)
  • Blue line: Thrust (N) - maximum thrust output
  • Red line: Specific Impulse (s) - fuel efficiency metric
  • Peak markers: Optimal operating points for maximum performance
  • Optimal range: LOX/RP-1: 2.5-2.8, LOX/LH2: 5.5-6.0, LOX/CH4: 3.6-3.8

What this shows: Engine performance variation with operating altitude

  • X-axis: Altitude (km) - from sea level to vacuum conditions
  • Y-axis: Performance parameters (Thrust, Isp, efficiency)
  • Blue curve: Thrust (kN) - decreases with altitude due to back pressure
  • Red curve: Specific Impulse (s) - increases toward vacuum Isp
  • Green curve: Nozzle efficiency - expansion efficiency at altitude
  • Reference lines: Sea level and vacuum performance markers

What this shows: 3D CAD visualization of liquid rocket engine assembly

  • Gray components: Engine structure, turbopumps, and plumbing
  • Blue/Cyan: Propellant tanks with internal anti-vortex baffles
  • Red/Orange: Combustion chamber and injector assembly
  • Purple gradient: Nozzle expansion cone with cooling channels
  • Interactive: Rotate (drag), zoom (scroll), pan (shift+drag)
  • Export options: STEP/STL files for CATIA/SolidWorks compatibility

What this shows: 3D engine assembly and component breakdown

  • Exploded view: Individual engine components separated for clarity
  • Color coding: Components grouped by subsystem (feed, ignition, cooling)
  • Flow paths: Propellant flow lines and cooling circuits
  • Mass breakdown: Component mass distribution and center of gravity
  • Manufacturing: Material specifications and assembly sequence
  • Quality control: Critical dimensions and tolerance requirements

What this shows: Mission trajectory analysis and optimization

  • X-axis: Time (s) from launch to orbit insertion
  • Y-axes: Multiple trajectory parameters (altitude, velocity, acceleration)
  • Blue line: Altitude profile (km) showing ascent trajectory
  • Red line: Velocity (km/s) build-up to orbital velocity
  • Green line: Acceleration (g) loading on vehicle and payload
  • Critical points: Stage separation, MECO, orbit insertion events
